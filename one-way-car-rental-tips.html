One-way car rental: tips for crew

Last updated June 2026 · about a 4-minute read

Flying into one city and out of another is normal life for crew — and it's exactly the situation where a one-way rental shines, letting you drive between points instead of backtracking. The catch is that one-way hires have their own quirks, the big one being the drop-off fee. Here's how to do them well.

Understand the one-way (drop-off) fee

When you return a car to a different location than you collected it, suppliers often add a one-way fee to cover repositioning the vehicle. It can range from modest to eye-watering depending on the route, the distance, and the countries involved. The key thing: this fee varies enormously between suppliers for the same route — which is exactly why comparing matters here more than usual.

Compare, because the fee swings wildly

On a one-way booking, two suppliers can quote completely different drop-off fees for the identical journey — one might bundle it in cheaply, another might charge a fortune. This is the rental scenario where shopping around delivers the biggest payoff, so don't grab the first option. A crew-focused comparison that pulls providers together makes this painless.

Cross-border adds rules, not just fees

Driving across a border one-way (dropping in a different country) often comes with extra conditions: higher fees, restrictions on which countries are allowed, and sometimes outright "no cross-border" rules on certain cars. If your one-way crosses a frontier, check the cross-border policy explicitly before booking, not at the counter.

One-way rule of thumb: the drop-off fee is the whole game. Compare providers for the same route, and always confirm cross-border rules if you're changing countries.

When one-way is worth it anyway

Even with a fee, a one-way can be the smart choice — it saves you doubling back, frees up time on a layover, and can be cheaper overall than the alternative of a round-trip rental plus separate transport. Weigh the drop-off fee against the time and hassle it saves, not against zero.

A few practical pointers

  • Book early — one-way inventory is more limited, so availability tightens sooner.
  • Keep it flexible — free amendment/cancellation matters even more when rosters can move.
  • Note the exact return location — big cities have multiple branches; returning to the wrong one can mean extra charges.

Frequently asked questions

Why is one-way car rental more expensive?+

Suppliers often add a one-way (drop-off) fee to cover repositioning the car back to its origin. The fee varies a lot by route and supplier, so comparing is key.

Can I drop a rental car in a different country?+

Sometimes, but cross-border one-ways come with extra fees and restrictions, and some cars aren't allowed across borders at all. Always check the cross-border policy before booking.

How do I get the cheapest one-way rate?+

Compare providers for the same route — drop-off fees swing widely between suppliers — and book early, since one-way inventory is more limited.
